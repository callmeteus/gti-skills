# Loss aversion

## Mechanism

Losing something feels stronger than gaining something of equal value, and people protect what they already have (status quo bias). An upgrade prompt that lists generic gains uses the weaker motivator. Showing what the user will concretely lose by doing nothing uses the stronger one.

## Bad screen

A storage app modal with a crown icon, `Upgrade to Premium`, `Get 100GB of storage and unlock premium features`, three generic benefits (`100GB storage space`, `Priority file sync`, `Advanced sharing options`), an `Upgrade Now` button, and `Maybe Later` below it. Nothing is at stake and nothing changes if the user dismisses it.

## Good screen (as shown in the video)

The same product and goal, framed as a loss:

- **Title:** `Your Files Will Be Deleted`.
- **Explanation:** `Storage is full. In 3 days, your oldest files will be permanently deleted to free up space.`
- **Files at risk:** `Project_Proposal.pdf`, `Design_Mockups.fig`, `and 12 other`, with `Total: 2.8 GB will be lost`.
- **Primary button:** `Protect My Files Now`.
- **Dismiss:** `I'll Risk It`.

## What to keep and what to change

Keep: naming the user's own items, the real size, and the real date of the consequence. Specific, personal facts are what make this screen work.

Change before recommending it:

- **Dismiss label:** `I'll Risk It` shames the user for declining (confirmshaming). Use a neutral label such as `Not now` or `Remind me tomorrow`.
- **The consequence must be true:** only show this framing when storage is actually full and files will actually be deleted or stop syncing on that date. If nothing will be lost, keep the gain framing.
- **Offer a free way out:** mention how to free space without paying when one exists, for example deleting large files.

## Rules

- Frame the ask around what the user already has and what inaction costs them, using their own data.
- State the real date or condition, not an invented countdown.
- Keep the decline option clear, neutral, and as easy to find as the primary button.

## Limits

- No fake timers, invented deadlines, exaggerated consequences, or threats about data that is not at risk.
- No confirmshaming in any dismiss or cancel label.
