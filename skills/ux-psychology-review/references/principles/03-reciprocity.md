# Reciprocity

## Mechanism

When someone receives something of value first, they feel a pull to give something back. A product that asks for an email, an account, or a payment before giving anything gets the opposite reaction: it feels like the result is being held hostage.

## Bad screen

A "Website Analyzer" titled `Get your free SEO report`. The user entered a URL, pressed analyze, and waited for the scan. The result is a blurred card with a lock and `Sign up to unlock`, followed by `Email` and `Password` fields and a `Create Account` button. The user has done the work and received nothing.

## Good screen

The user presses analyze and gets a real, useful report right away:

- **Header:** `Your SEO Report` with a `Free` badge.
- **Score:** `Overall SEO Score 72/100` and `Good - 16 issues found`.
- **Findings:** `3 Critical Issues` (missing meta descriptions, broken links), `5 Warnings` (slow images, missing alt text), and `8 Passed` (mobile-friendly, encrypted data).
- **Prompt at the bottom:** `Want the full 50-page report?` with `Save your results and get fix-by-fix instructions` and a `Save My Report - Free` button.

Signup now saves something the user already has, instead of unlocking something they have been denied. Free trials and products usable before payment apply the same idea.

## Rules

- Deliver a meaningful result before asking for an account, email, or payment.
- Place the ask after the value, framed as saving or extending what the user received.
- Make the free part useful on its own: the user should be able to act on it.

## Limits

- Do not make the user wait for or work toward a result and then withhold it. That is the bad screen.
- The free part must be genuine, not a teaser stripped of every useful detail.
- Describe exactly what the account adds ("full 50-page report", "fix-by-fix instructions") so the ask is honest.
