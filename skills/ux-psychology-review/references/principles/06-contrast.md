# Contrast

## Mechanism

People judge a number relative to the number they saw just before it. A cost shown alone is judged in absolute terms and often converted into a larger total in the user's head. The same cost shown next to a larger, related amount feels small. The first number on screen becomes the ruler for everything after it.

## Bad screen

A protection plan on its own page: a feature list ending in `24/7 priority technical support`, a price box `$411.14 /year` with `Cancel anytime`, an `Add AppleCare+` button, and `No thanks` below. The narration describes the same scene as $50 a month, but the screen shows the annual price. The user sees the cost in isolation and declines.

## Good screen (as shown in the video)

An `Order Review` screen. The product comes first: `MacBook Pro 14"`, `M3 Pro - 18GB RAM - 512GB SSD`, `$1,899`. Directly below, under `RECOMMENDED FOR YOU`, a `Complete Protection` card reads `Full coverage for damage, theft, and repairs`, `$49 /month`, a green `Just 2.6%` label, and an `Add` button. The order summary below shows `Subtotal $1,899.00`, `Shipping Free`, `Total $1,899`.

## What to keep and what to change

Keep: placing the add-on in context next to the purchase it protects, so the user compares it to the related price instead of nothing.

Change before recommending it:

- **Like-for-like comparison:** `Just 2.6%` compares a monthly recurring fee with a one-time price. Over a year, `$49/month` is `$588`, about 31% of `$1,899`. Compare like with like: show the yearly total next to the product price, or the monthly fee next to a monthly figure.
- **Show the commitment:** state the billing period and the yearly total near the price, not only in the fine print.
- **Optional by default:** the add-on starts unselected and the total does not include it until the user adds it, as in the screen above.

## Rules

- Never show a cost in isolation when a relevant reference exists: the product it attaches to, the plan it upgrades from, or the cost of the problem it prevents.
- Control the order: put the reference point first and the cost after it.
- When showing tiers, order them so the recommended plan sits next to a higher one, and make every tier a real option.

## Limits

- No decoy prices that no one can buy, no inflated "original" prices, and no comparisons across different billing periods.
- Percentages and savings must be calculated on the same basis as the price they describe.
