# Originality

References set the level. They never set the layout.

When the user shows references, or when you think of a familiar post, extract what makes it good (the idea, the hero, depth, type contrast, photo treatment) and build something new with those qualities. A piece that a viewer could match to one specific reference is a failure, even if it looks good.

## Rules

- **Never clone:** do not reproduce a reference's composition, copy, palette, and subject together. Change at least the structure, the palette, and the type pairing.
- **Combine:** build each variant from at least two of the structures in [composition.md](composition.md), or one structure with an unusual hero treatment.
- **Derive the palette from the brand:** start from the user's colors or the subject's colors, then apply the palette strategy (one field, one hot accent, one neutral).
- **Write new copy:** never reuse a reference's text, brand, or slogan. Write for the user's offer in the user's language.
- **Vary the set:** when you deliver two to four variants, no two share the same idea, hero, palette, and display family; and none repeats the idea or display family of the last art in the chat (`history --json`).
- **Do not copy yourself:** your own earlier pieces are references too. The default moves in [visual-language.md](visual-language.md) are what your pieces share when you stop deciding.
- **Templates only on request:** `generate --template` is for repeatable formats the user asked for (a weekly carousel, a price card series). For a new piece, write original HTML.
- **Respect rights:** no third-party logos, brand names, or recognizable people from references. Use the user's assets or openly licensed photos.

## Self-check before handing over

- [ ] Could someone point to the reference this was copied from? If yes, change the structure.
- [ ] Can you say the idea in one line ("this piece is a ___")? Is it different from the other variants' ideas?
- [ ] Does a hero picture fill 40 to 70 percent of the board, and does the type touch it?
- [ ] Six elements or fewer, each large? Does the audit report `house-style` with two or more default moves? If so, cut them.
- [ ] Is the headline 6 to 12 times the supporting text, in a display family chosen for the idea?
- [ ] Does the palette start from the brand or the photo, rather than near-black with a glow?
- [ ] Is every ornament attached to what it is about, and could you say why it is there?
- [ ] Does the piece use the whole canvas, with intentional bleed and cropping?
- [ ] Would it sit next to a strong professional feed without looking cheaper?
