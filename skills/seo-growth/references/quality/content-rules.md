# Content rules

Every page or section the agent drafts must pass these rules before it is shown to the user or placed in the repository.

## Proof

- Every claim of fact (a number, a price, a feature, a comparison, a customer result) has a source: the codebase, the user's data, a public page checked today, or the user's own words.
- Never invent statistics, quotes, testimonials, reviews, case studies, author bios, or credentials. Where proof is missing, write `[NEEDS PROOF: what is needed]` and list every marker in the report.
- A page needs at least 4 of these 5 kinds of proof: first-hand data or results, screenshots or examples of the real product, named sources or citations, specific numbers with dates, and an expert or author with relevant experience.
- Competitor facts are checked on the competitor's own site on the day of writing, and dated on the page ("Pricing checked on 2026-11-20").

## Answer first

- The direct answer to the page's main question is in the first 40 words. Context comes after.
- Each H2 that is a question is answered in the first sentence below it.
- Write the introduction last, once the body is done.

## Intent and structure

- One page, one primary keyword, one intent (see the keyword map).
- Headings describe their sections. Short paragraphs, lists and tables where they help a reader compare.
- Include what the reader needs to act: price, next step, limits, who it is not for.

## Plain writing

Strip the patterns that make text read as generated and that readers and AI answers skip:

- Empty openers and closers: "In today's fast-paced world", "In conclusion", "Let's dive in", "It's important to note".
- Inflated words: "unlock", "elevate", "seamless", "robust", "game-changer", "revolutionize", "leverage" (as a verb), "delve", "landscape", "realm", "tapestry".
- Vague claims without a number or example: "many users", "significantly faster", "industry-leading".
- Groups of three adjectives, rhetorical questions as headings when a statement works, and the same sentence shape repeated.
- Em-dash chains and exclamation marks.

Read the draft once looking only for these, and rewrite each hit as a concrete statement.

## Honesty

- Comparison and alternatives pages include where the competitor wins.
- No fake urgency, fake scarcity, or fake update dates. Change `dateModified` only when the content changes.
- Affiliate or sponsored relationships are disclosed on the page.
- No keyword stuffing, hidden text, or content shown to crawlers but not to users.
